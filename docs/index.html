Meet the mascot

Vigi is always on watch

A small shield who blinks, glances around, waves, and shows up the moment a blocked app gets caught — not a nag, just a friendly redirect.

She reacts to more than just blocking

In the optional Quickshell bar widget, pixel-art Vigi keeps tabs on what's actually happening on your screen — independent of whether FocusGuard is blocking anything.

  • 🎵 Music grooves faster when any MPRIS player is playing
  • 👀 Alert flashes amber and shakes when a distracting app is focused
  • 😳 Stressed turns pink and blinks fast when your CPU is under load
  • 😴 Asleep eyes shut, motion stops, after the screen's been idle a while
  • 🐾 Petting left-click for a wiggle and a random reaction line
What it does

Everything a focus mode should be

Not a honor-system timer. Not built for a desktop you're not running.

🖥️

Native GTK4 + libadwaita

Not a web page in a window. A real, polished Linux desktop app.

🔍

Real app discovery

Via Gio.DesktopAppInfo, with icons, search, Select All / Clear All.

📅

Schedules

Days of week, start/end time — correctly handles windows that cross midnight.

⚡

Manual sessions

"Start School now for 45 minutes" from the GUI or CLI, no schedule needed.

🧩

Multiple profiles

School, Study, Gaming, Bedtime — each with its own apps, schedule, and duration.

Why not an existing tool?

Timers don't enforce anything

ToolWhy it doesn't fit
Timekpr-nExTManages session time budgets — no concept of blocking individual apps while you're logged in.
MalcontentBuilt around Flatpak's permission store and GNOME session services — a poor fit for a minimal Hyprland setup.
cgroups aloneCan throttle a process group but can't stop a new one from launching without root + a BPF LSM filter.

So FocusGuard does what this class of tool has always done on Linux without root: a fast poll-and-kill loop over /proc, matched against exact executable signatures.
